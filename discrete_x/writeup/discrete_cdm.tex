\begin{abstract}
We extend Conditional Distribution Matching (CDM) to \emph{discrete} inputs:
find a prompt $x^\ast$ such that the induced conditional
$\Pd(Y \mid X{=}x^\ast)$ of a frozen text-to-image model matches a target
distribution $\G(Y)$. Gradients with respect to $x$ do not exist, so we
replace MLGD's gradient correction with \emph{selection}: a twisted
Sequential Monte Carlo sampler over a masked (absorbing) discrete diffusion
prior, in which the distributional loss enters only as a scalar. Three
additions make the method work at scale: (i) \emph{cross-modal tilted
proposals} --- a CLIP-text surrogate nominates and ranks candidate tokens so
that rare target-relevant words are actually proposed; (ii) a
\emph{remasking corrector} that lets the chain revise committed tokens; and
(iii) a non-distilled conditional sampler, which we show is \emph{necessary}
for multimodal targets because adversarially distilled models (SDXL-Turbo)
collapse linguistic ambiguity to a single mode. With the full stack, a
multimodal target defined by the prompt ``an old man or young woman
smiling'' is recovered from a 75\%-masked corruption in 5/6 runs up to the
estimation noise floor of the objective, with induced demographics matching
the target.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem}
CDM \emph{(main paper)} seeks an input whose induced conditional matches a
user-specified target distribution. Here the input space is discrete: $x$
is a token sequence (a prompt), the conditional model is a frozen
text-to-image generator $f_\phi$, and we seek
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:objective}
x^\ast \;=\; \arg\min_x \Ploss(x), \qquad
\Ploss(x) \;=\; \mathrm{MMD}^2\!\big( \{ \mathrm{CLIP}(y_j) \}_{j=1}^{n_{\mathrm{cond}}},\; S_\G \big),
\quad y_j \sim f_\phi(x, \cdot),
\end{equation}
where $S_\G$ is a fixed set of CLIP embeddings of target images
($n_{\mathrm{cond}}{=}8$ with common random numbers so $\Ploss$ is a
deterministic, memoizable function of $x$). As in the main paper we target
the tempered posterior
$\mathcal{Q}_\beta(x) \propto \Pd(x)\, e^{-\beta \Ploss(x)}$.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Setup and notation}
\label{sec:notation}
Let $V$ be a vocabulary of $A$ word tokens and $\mask \notin V$ a
distinguished \textsc{mask} symbol. A \emph{canvas} is a fixed-length
sequence $x = (x^1, \dots, x^{L}) \in (V \cup \{\mask\})^{L}$; we call a
canvas \emph{decoded} if no position carries $\mask$. The prompt handed to
the image model is $\dec(x)$: a fixed natural-language prefix (e.g.\ ``a
photo of'') followed by the canvas words. $V$ is the intersection of the
masked LM's alphabetic word tokens with CLIP's single-token words
($A \approx 18\mathrm{k}$ for BERT), so that one canvas slot is exactly one
CLIP text token --- this alignment is what makes the cross-modal proposals
of \S\ref{sec:tilt} possible.

\paragraph{The loss oracle.} For a decoded canvas, $\Ploss$ in
\eqref{eq:objective} is computed by generating $n_{\mathrm{cond}}{=}8$
images $y_j \sim f_\phi(\dec(x), \cdot)$ with \emph{common random numbers}
(the same $n_{\mathrm{cond}}$ initial latents for every prompt), embedding
them with CLIP ViT-L/14, and evaluating the biased (V-statistic) squared
maximum mean discrepancy against the target set
$S_\G = \{s_1,\dots,s_{n_{\mathrm{targ}}}\}$, $n_{\mathrm{targ}}{=}64$:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{MMD}^2(E, S_\G)
= \frac{1}{n^2}\!\sum_{j,j'} k(e_j, e_{j'})
+ \frac{1}{m^2}\!\sum_{l,l'} k(s_l, s_{l'})
- \frac{2}{nm}\!\sum_{j,l} k(e_j, s_l),
\qquad
k(u,v) = e^{-\gamma \|u - v\|^2},
\end{equation}
with the RBF bandwidth set by the median heuristic on the target set,
$\gamma^{-1} = \mathrm{median}\{\|s_l - s_{l'}\|^2\}_{l<l'}$. Common random
numbers make $\Ploss$ a \emph{deterministic function of the prompt string},
so evaluations are memoized and repeated queries are free. Nothing
downstream uses gradients of $\Ploss$; it is consumed strictly as a scalar
(a ``loss oracle'').

\paragraph{Goal.} Sample from the tempered posterior
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tempered}
\mathcal{Q}_\beta(x_0) \;\propto\; \Pd(x_0)\, e^{-\beta \Ploss(x_0)},
\end{equation}
where $\Pd$ is a prior over decoded canvases (defined next) and
$\beta > 0$ is an inverse temperature ($\beta{=}200$ throughout). As
$\beta \to \infty$, $\mathcal{Q}_\beta$ concentrates on the minimizers of
$\Ploss$ within the support of $\Pd$; the prior's role is to restrict the
search to fluent English rather than adversarial token strings.

\subsection{The prior: masked (absorbing) discrete diffusion}
\label{sec:prior}
\paragraph{Forward process.} Discrete times $t = 0, 1, \dots, T$
($T{=}16$). Given a decoded canvas $x_0$, the forward process masks each
position independently:
\begin{equation}
q(x_t \mid x_0) = \prod_{i=1}^{L} q(x_t^i \mid x_0^i),
\qquad
q(x_t^i = \mask \mid x_0^i) = \tfrac{t}{T},
\quad
q(x_t^i = x_0^i \mid x_0^i) = 1 - \tfrac{t}{T},
\end{equation}
so $x_T$ is all-masked and information is destroyed by \emph{absorption}
only --- a token is either intact or masked, never substituted.

\paragraph{Denoiser.} The learned object is the conditional
$p_\theta(x_0^i = v \mid x_t)$ for masked positions $i$, i.e.\ ``which word
sits under this mask given the visible context''. This is \emph{exactly}
the masked-language-modelling objective, so an off-the-shelf MLM
(\texttt{bert-base-uncased}) is a legitimate denoiser without any training;
we also use LLaDA-8B, a diffusion LM trained explicitly with this
objective. Both expose the same interface: per-position marginals
$p_\theta(\cdot \mid x_t) \in \Delta(V)$ over the restricted vocabulary.

\paragraph{Reverse kernel.} One reverse step $r_\theta(x_{t-1} \mid x_t)$
acts independently per position: an \emph{unmasked} position is copied
unchanged; a \emph{masked} position unmasks with probability $1/t$, and if
it unmasks, its word is drawn from the denoiser marginal truncated to its
top-$k$ ($k{=}50$, renormalized --- standard top-$k$ decoding; this
truncation modifies the prior we sample, not the correctness of the
weighting below). The rate $1/t$ makes the reverse marginals consistent
with the forward ones: a position masked at time $t$ survives to time
$t-j$ with probability $\prod_{s=t-j+1}^{t}(1 - \tfrac1s) = \tfrac{t-j}{t}$,
and at $t{=}1$ everything unmasks, so the chain always terminates decoded.
The prior $\Pd(x_0)$ in \eqref{eq:tempered} is \emph{defined} as the law of
$x_0$ under running $r_\theta$ from the all-masked canvas.

\subsection{Guidance: a Feynman--Kac model solved by twisted SMC}
\label{sec:smc}
We cannot evaluate $\Pd(x_0)$ or its gradients; what we \emph{can} do is
simulate the reverse chain and query the scalar oracle $\Ploss$. This is
precisely the setting of Feynman--Kac (FK) models: reweight a simulable
Markov chain by potential functions so that its \emph{final} state is
distributed according to a twisted target.

\paragraph{Optimal twist.} Define, for each time $t$, the value function
(``twist'')
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:twist}
h_t(x_t) \;=\; \E\!\left[ e^{-\beta \Ploss(x_0)} \,\middle|\, x_t \right],
\end{equation}
the expectation being over the remainder of the reverse chain
$x_{t-1}, \dots, x_0 \sim r_\theta$ started at $x_t$. Two properties
matter:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{Terminal exactness}: a decoded canvas has no remaining
randomness, so $h_0(x_0) = e^{-\beta \Ploss(x_0)}$ exactly.
\item[(ii)] \emph{Tower property}: by the law of total expectation,
$\E_{r_\theta}\!\left[ h_{t-1}(x_{t-1}) \mid x_t \right] = h_t(x_t)$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The FK path targets.} For the chain started at $t_0$ (see
\S\ref{sec:sdedit} for the start distribution $\mu$), define unnormalized
path densities
\begin{equation}
\gamma_t(x_{t_0:t}) \;=\;
\mu(x_{t_0}) \prod_{s=t}^{t_0-1} r_\theta(x_{s} \mid x_{s+1})
\;\cdot\; h_t(x_t),
\qquad t = t_0, \dots, 0 .
\end{equation}
At $t{=}0$, by (i), the $x_0$-marginal of $\gamma_0$ is proportional to
$\Pd(x_0)\, e^{-\beta \Ploss(x_0)} = \mathcal{Q}_\beta(x_0)$ --- the target
\eqref{eq:tempered}. Sequential importance sampling on this sequence, with
the prior kernel $r_\theta$ as proposal, gives the incremental weight
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:incw}
\alpha_t(x_t, x_{t-1})
\;=\;
\frac{\gamma_{t-1}(x_{t_0:t-1})}
     {\gamma_{t}(x_{t_0:t})\; r_\theta(x_{t-1} \mid x_t)}
\;=\;
\frac{h_{t-1}(x_{t-1})}{h_t(x_t)} ,
\end{equation}
because everything except the twists cancels. By (ii) these weights have
conditional mean one, and with the \emph{exact} twist they are the
minimum-variance choice (the ``optimal twist'' of the SMC literature).

\paragraph{The algorithm.} $N{=}128$ particles $\{x^n\}$ with log-weights
$\{\log w^n\}$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Init} ($t = t_0$): draw $x^n \sim \mu$, set
$\log w^n = \log \hat h_{t_0}(x^n)$.
\item \textbf{Propagate}: $x^n_{t-1} \sim r_\theta(\cdot \mid x^n_t)$
(or the tilted proposal of \S\ref{sec:tilt}, followed by the corrector of
\S\ref{sec:corrector}).
\item \textbf{Reweight}:
$\log w^n \mathrel{+}= \log \hat h_{t-1}(x^n_{t-1}) - \log \hat h_{t}(x^n_{t})
\;[\,+ \log r_\theta - \log q \text{ if tilted}\,]$.
\item \textbf{Resample}: with normalized weights
$W^n = e^{\log w^n} / \sum_m e^{\log w^m}$, compute the effective sample
size $\mathrm{ESS} = 1 / \sum_n (W^n)^2$ (the number of ``useful''
particles: $N$ when weights are uniform, $1$ when one particle carries all
mass). If $\mathrm{ESS} < N/2$, resample systematically (duplicate
high-weight particles, kill low-weight ones, preserving expectations) and
reset $\log w^n = 0$.
\item Loop $t \to t-1$ until $t{=}0$; report the best decoded particle
by $\Ploss$ (optimization view) or the weighted ensemble (sampling view).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Plug-in twist estimators.} The exact $h_t$ of \eqref{eq:twist}
is intractable; we substitute a plug-in $\hat h_t$ built from the
denoiser's factorized posterior
$\tilde p_\theta(x_0 \mid x_t) = \prod_{i:\, x_t^i = \mask}
p_\theta(x_0^i \mid x_t)$ (independent fills --- itself an approximation,
since true fills are correlated):
\begin{align}
\textbf{mode:}\quad
& \xhat(x_t)^i =
\begin{cases}
x_t^i & x_t^i \neq \mask\\
\arg\max_v p_\theta(v \mid x_t) & \text{else}
\end{cases},
& \hat h_t(x_t) &= e^{-\beta_t \Ploss(\dec(\xhat))},
\\[2pt]
\textbf{sampled:}\quad
& x_0^{(j)} \sim \tilde p_\theta(\cdot \mid x_t),\; j = 1{:}n_{\mathrm{dec}},
& \hat h_t(x_t) &= \frac{1}{n_{\mathrm{dec}}}
\sum_{j=1}^{n_{\mathrm{dec}}} e^{-\beta_t \Ploss(\dec(x_0^{(j)}))}.
\end{align}
Mode costs one oracle call per particle per step; sampled costs
$n_{\mathrm{dec}}$ (we use $4$--$8$) and is unbiased for the twist
\emph{under the factorized posterior} --- the residual bias is exactly the
factorization. Crucially, plug-in error cannot corrupt the final target:
the reweighting telescopes,
\begin{equation}
\sum_{t=1}^{t_0} \big[ \log \hat h_{t-1} - \log \hat h_t \big]
+ \log \hat h_{t_0}
\;=\; \log \hat h_0 \;=\; -\beta\, \Ploss(x_0),
\end{equation}
and $\hat h_0$ is exact by terminal exactness (i). Intermediate twist error
therefore only shifts \emph{where resampling focuses compute} (variance /
efficiency), not the distribution the weights ultimately encode --- the
standard consistency argument for twisted SMC with approximate twists.

\paragraph{$\beta$-annealing.} We use the schedule
$\beta_t = \beta\,(T - t)/T$: zero guidance at $t{=}T$, full $\beta$ at
$t{=}0$. Rationale: early in the chain most positions are masked, so
$\xhat$ is dominated by the denoiser's unconditional guesses and
$\Ploss(\xhat)$ is nearly uninformative about the particle --- full-strength
weighting there just resamples on noise and collapses diversity before any
signal exists. Since $\beta_0 = \beta$, the final target is still
$\mathcal{Q}_\beta$. Annealing was essential in every setting we tested
(without it, the toy benchmark drops from 5/5 to 0/5 recovery).

\subsection{SDEdit-style initialization (editing and recovery)}
\label{sec:sdedit}
Rather than starting all-masked ($\mu = \delta_{\text{all-}\mask}$,
$t_0 = T$), every particle starts from a \emph{source} canvas
$x_{\mathrm{src}}$ with each position independently re-masked w.p.\
$t_0/T$ --- an exact draw from the forward corruption
$q(x_{t_0} \mid x_{\mathrm{src}})$ --- and the reverse chain runs from
$t_0$ ($t_0/T = 0.75$ throughout). Because particles now differ at
initialization, the correct initial FK weight is the twist itself,
$\log w^n = \log \hat h_{t_0}(x^n)$ (for the identical all-masked start
this is a constant and reduces to uniform weights, so from-scratch
behavior is unchanged). The \emph{recovery} task sets
$x_{\mathrm{src}} = x_{\mathrm{true}}$ (denoise your way back); the
\emph{editing} task sets it to a semantically different prompt.

\subsection{Cross-modal tilted proposals}
\label{sec:tilt}
Bootstrap SMC proposes from the prior and lets weights select --- but
selection cannot select what is never proposed. At vocabulary scale the
denoiser's marginals concentrate on generic words; target-relevant rare
words (``rain'') sit at rank ${\sim}5{,}000$ of $18\mathrm{k}$ and are
effectively never drawn by top-$k$ sampling. We therefore \emph{tilt} the
proposal toward the target using a differentiable surrogate, and pay for
it exactly in the weights.

\paragraph{Surrogate.} Let
$\bar s_\G = \frac{1}{|S_\G|}\sum_l s_l \big/ \|\cdot\|$ be the normalized
mean target embedding and
\begin{equation}
\Lsurr(x) \;=\; 1 - \cos\!\big( \mathrm{CLIP}_{\mathrm{text}}(\dec(x)),\;
\bar s_\G \big),
\end{equation}
a \emph{text-side} proxy for the image-side loss: no image generation, one
text-encoder forward. CLIP's shared text--image embedding space is what
makes comparing a \emph{prompt} embedding to \emph{image} embeddings
meaningful; this is the cross-modal step.

\paragraph{Step 1 --- gradient nomination.} Write the canvas one-hot:
slot $i$ carries $q_i \in \Delta(V)$, and the CLIP text embedding of slot
$i$ is $q_i^\top E$ with $E \in \mathbb{R}^{A \times d}$ the token
embedding matrix. Relaxing $q_i$ to the simplex makes $\Lsurr$
differentiable in $(q_1,\dots,q_L)$; \emph{one} backprop at the current
mode-decode $\xhat$ yields
$g_{i,v} = \partial \Lsurr / \partial q_{i,v}$ for \emph{all} slots $i$
and words $v$ simultaneously --- a first-order estimate of the effect of
substituting word $v$ into slot $i$ (Gibbs-with-Gradients style). This
gradient is used \emph{only to nominate}: candidate pool
$C_i = \mathrm{top}\text{-}k\big(p_\theta(\cdot \mid x_t)\big) \cup
\mathrm{top}\text{-}k(-g_{i,\cdot})$, $k = 50$.

\paragraph{Step 2 --- exact scoring.} For each nominee $v \in C_i$,
evaluate $\Lsurr(\xhat^{\,i \leftarrow v})$ --- where
$\xhat^{\,i \leftarrow v}$ denotes the mode decode with slot $i$ replaced
by word $v$ --- \emph{exactly}, with one batched
CLIP-text forward over the pool ($|C_i| \le 100$ prompts). No Taylor
approximation survives into the numbers used for sampling.

\paragraph{Step 3 --- tilted fill and exact weight correction.} A position
that unmasks draws its word from
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tiltq}
q_i(v) \;\propto\;
p_\theta(v \mid x_t)\;
e^{-\lambda\, \beta_{t-1}\, \Lsurr(\xhat^{\,i \leftarrow v})},
\qquad v \in C_i,
\end{equation}
with tilt strength $\lambda{=}1$. Changing the proposal from $r_\theta$ to
$q$ changes the denominator of \eqref{eq:incw}, so each tilted fill
contributes the exact correction
$\log p_\theta(v \mid x_t) - \log q_i(v)$ to the particle's log-weight
(these accumulate over filled positions within the step). Consequence:
\emph{a biased surrogate can bias only efficiency, never the target} ---
if $\Lsurr$ nominates junk, the weights pay for it and resampling discards
it. The one caveat is support: $q$ puts no mass outside $C_i$, comparable
in kind to the top-$k$ truncation already present in the baseline kernel.

A pure first-order tilt over the full vocabulary
($q(v) \propto p_\theta(v) e^{-\lambda \beta g_{i,v}}$, the published
D-CBG recipe) \emph{fails} here --- loss increases with tilt strength ---
because the Taylor error at the distance of a full one-hot swap is larger
than the signal. Nominate-with-gradients, score-exactly is what works.
Since the surrogate lives entirely in text space, its overhead at image
scale is ${\sim}25\%$ of wall-clock.

\subsection{Remasking corrector}
\label{sec:corrector}
Absorbing diffusion can never revise an unmasked token --- whatever is
committed (from the source prompt or an early bad fill) survives verbatim
unless re-masked. We observed exactly this failure: corrupted source
tokens surviving into final answers. The corrector inserts, after each
propagation step, an independent re-masking of every \emph{unmasked}
position with probability
\begin{equation}
\sigma_t \;=\; \sigma\, \frac{t-1}{T}, \qquad \sigma = 0.2,
\end{equation}
which decays to $\sigma_1 = 0$ so the chain still terminates decoded. The
composite kernel (unmask-fill $\circ$ re-mask) is treated as \emph{the}
proposal kernel of the FK model: the telescoping identity of
\S\ref{sec:smc} is agnostic to which kernel generated the path, so the
final weights still encode $e^{-\beta \Ploss(x_0)}$ against the law of
$x_0$ under the \emph{corrector-augmented} chain. That law is a
(slightly) different effective prior than pure absorbing diffusion ---
ReMDM-style schedules choose $\sigma_t$ to preserve the prior marginals
exactly; our fixed decaying schedule treats this as an approximation, which
we accept in exchange for reversibility. Empirically the corrector took
the text-space rain benchmark from a $0.24$ plateau to \emph{exact
recovery} ($\Ploss = 0.0000$) and produced the compositional straddle of
\S\ref{sec:headline}.

\section{Experiments}
All image-scale runs: SDXL (Turbo 1-step or base 12-step CFG~3) as
$f_\phi$, CLIP ViT-L/14 embeddings, $N{=}128$, $T{=}16$, $\beta{=}200$
annealed, seeds $\{0,1,2\}$, single L40S, minutes per run (mode
estimator).

\subsection{Validation ladder}
\textbf{Toy world} (5-slot grammar, 3{,}750 prompts, exact denoiser,
brute-forceable optimum): with $\beta$-annealing the \emph{sampled}
estimator recovers the generating prompt 5/5 seeds; \emph{mode} 1/5 (its
deterministic twist is biased and all failures collapse to the same wrong
prompt). \textbf{At image scale the ranking flips}: mode $\geq$ sampled on
every seed (e.g.\ rain pair: mean $0.454$ vs $0.479$) at $1/12$ the cost
--- greedy decodes are coherent sentences, factorized samples are not.
Mode is the working configuration.

\subsection{Prompt editing (unimodal target)}
Target $\G$: images of \emph{``a photo of a person standing in the rain''}
($\Ploss(x_{\mathrm{true}}) = 0.072$); source ``a photo of a man walking
down the street'' ($\Ploss = 0.80$). Selection alone inserts rain
semantics once in three seeds (best $0.393$); tilted proposals make rain
vocabulary appear in 2/3 seeds (best $0.373$, ``man in precipitation
water''); both plateau because 5 free tokens cannot express composition
--- the target is \emph{distant full-body} silhouettes. Widening the
canvas to 10 tokens breaks the plateau: $\Ploss = 0.189$
(10-token optimum $0.095$), with generations visually interchangeable
with the target. Across 18 baseline runs and 3 source--target pairs,
untilted SMC reliably performs \emph{high-prior} semantic swaps
(man$\to$woman: 6/6) and never inserts \emph{low-prior} content words ---
the coverage bottleneck that the tilt fixes.

\subsection{Distillation collapses ambiguity}
\label{sec:distill}
Defining $\G$ as a 50/50 mixture of ``old man smiling'' / ``young woman
smiling'' images: under SDXL-\textbf{Turbo}, all seeds converge to the
identical single-mode prompt (``a photo of a woman smiling in the image'',
induced $p(\mathrm{male}){=}0.01$ vs target $0.49$), and a probe over
hand-crafted straddle prompts shows why: \emph{no} prompt beats the
single-mode floor; disjunctions \emph{blend} attributes (old, mixed-gender
people) rather than sampling modes; ``a person smiling'' induces
$p(\mathrm{male}) = 0.007$. Under non-distilled SDXL-\textbf{base}
(12 steps, CFG 3) the picture inverts: disjunction prompts \emph{beat}
both components ($0.086$ vs $0.090/0.097$) and ``a person smiling'' jumps
to $p(\mathrm{male}) = 0.56$. Re-running the mixture optimization with the
base sampler, recovered prompts induce genuinely mixed demographics
($p(\mathrm{male}) \in \{0.26, 0.78\}$ vs target $0.54$). Because the SMC
never differentiates through $f_\phi$, this sampler swap is architecturally
free --- in contrast to MLGD-F, whose memory tractability requires the
distilled model. \emph{Selection-based guidance decouples CDM from
distilled samplers.}

$\G$ = its own 64 images; $\Ploss(x_{\mathrm{true}}) = 0.099$ is the
finite-sample noise floor of the objective. Particles start from
$x_{\mathrm{true}}$ with 75\% of tokens masked (classic denoising setup).

\subsection{Equal-compute baselines}
\label{sec:baselines}
Each baseline receives $\sim$250 unique loss-oracle evaluations per seed,
matched to (or above) the guided runs' consumption, and shares the
memoized loss. \emph{SDEdit best-of-$B$}: $B{=}256$ independent
\emph{unguided} denoisings from the same noised source (same prior, same
$t_0$, same top-$k$ reverse kernel --- everything except guidance), keep
the lowest-loss decode. \emph{Random search}: $B{=}256$ uniform-random
canvas fills.

\subsection{A rate-based alternative: training-free CTMC guidance}
\label{sec:ctmc}
Twisted SMC is not the only way to consume a scalar oracle. Rate-ratio
guidance (Nisonoff et al.; TFG-Flow) works on the continuous-time Markov
chain directly: a \emph{single} chain, no particles, no weights, with the
predictor $f(x_1) = e^{-\beta \Ploss(x_1)}$ reweighting each unmasking
jump. For a masked coordinate $d$ unmasking at time $s$, the guided
destination law is
\begin{equation}
q_d(v) \;\propto\;
\E_{x_1 \sim \tilde p_\theta(\cdot \mid x_s)}
\!\big[ f(x_1)\, \mathbf{1}\{x_1^d = v\} \big],
\end{equation}
estimated either by \textbf{Monte Carlo} ($K$ posterior decodes per step,
$f$-weighted empirical law --- assumption-free, and attractive precisely
because it needs \emph{only} the scalar oracle: no CLIP-text surrogate, no
gradients, no shared embedding space), or in \textbf{closed form} under
the relaxing assumption that $f$ is log-linear across coordinates
($\log f = \sum_d \theta_d(x^d)$; then $q_d = \mathrm{softmax}(\log
p_\theta + \gamma\,\theta_d)$, one denoiser pass, zero sampling).

\textbf{In the toy world the method matches SMC}: MC rate guidance with
$K{=}128$, $\gamma{=}10$, 16 restarts recovers the generating prompt 5/5
seeds at ${\sim}9\times$ fewer raw oracle calls than the SMC toy
configuration. The closed-form variant plateaus ($\Ploss \approx 0.08$ vs
floor $0.009$) and \emph{degrades} as $\gamma$ grows: a
distribution-matching loss couples coordinates by construction, so its
additive projection is a poor surrogate --- the assumption's own scope
caveat, measured.

\textbf{At image scale the ranking inverts} (same tasks, matched
${\sim}250$ unique oracle calls per seed):

Three observations pin down \emph{why}. First, $\gamma{=}3$ vs
$\gamma{=}10$ barely differ --- guidance strength is not the binding
constraint. Second, reallocating the budget toward sharper per-step
estimates ($K{=}64$, 2 chains) makes things \emph{worse} than $K{=}16$
with 4 chains: better local twist estimates cannot help a chain that has
already committed a bad token, whereas extra restarts can. Third --- and
this addresses the natural objection that a single chain \emph{can} be
made revisable --- we added the DFM detailed-balance corrector (Campbell
et al.\ 2024): any rate $R^{\mathrm{DB}}$ in detailed balance with the
conditional marginals can be added at strength $\eta$ without changing
the prior's marginal flow, which for masking means unmasked coordinates
re-mask at rate $\eta$ while the unmask rate grows to
$(\dot\kappa + \eta\kappa)/(1-\kappa)$. In the toy this lifts MC rate
guidance to 5/5 recovery ($\eta{=}8$). At image scale its effect is
\emph{symmetric in the wrong way}: editing improves ($0.165 \to 0.143$,
the best non-SMC editing result --- revision does help escape a bad
source) but recovery \emph{degrades} ($0.111 \to 0.136$), because the
same uniform churn that erases a bad initialization erases a good one ---
at $\eta{=}8$ the recovery and editing chains become nearly
indistinguishable, the initialization having been fully forgotten.
Detailed-balance exploration is revision without \emph{selection}: it
re-masks good and bad tokens at the same rate. SMC's resampling is the
selective version of the same operation --- states die \emph{because} of
their loss --- and that is the mechanism a single guided chain has no
analogue of. We then tested whether that selectivity can be retrofitted
at zero oracle cost: a \emph{regret-guided} remask keeps the same total
churn as uniform $\eta{=}8$ but allocates it by softmax of each
coordinate's commit regret --- the mean loss of the decodes that chose
its committed token minus the mean over all refills, both cached for
free from the $K$ decodes at its commit step --- so tokens that
underperformed their own refill distribution re-mask preferentially
(rate-ratio guidance applied to the remask jumps). It preserves the
toy's 5/5 recovery, but at image scale it is indistinguishable from
uniform churn: editing identical ($0.143$), recovery marginally better
($0.136 \to 0.131$) yet still worse than no revision at all ($0.111$),
and the recovery and editing trajectories still coincide. The failure
localizes the problem precisely: the regret statistics are \emph{stale}
--- they score a token against the canvas as it stood at commit time,
not as it stands now --- whereas resampling always scores whole
\emph{current} states. Selective single-chain revision would need fresh
counterfactual twist estimates per unmasked coordinate at every step,
exactly the cost that resampling amortizes across a population.
Consistent with this,
TFG-Flow's own benchmarks live in small alphabets (DNA: 4, proteins: 20),
where per-step decodes cover the space and the toy result shows the
method at its best. The closed-form arm is not useless --- one seed
produced ``an elderly man or young woman smiling'' ($\Ploss{=}0.109$,
demographics $0.63/0.88$ vs target $0.60/0.84$) at \emph{four}
sampling-time oracle calls --- but on average the additive assumption
costs more than the exactness buys.

\section{Findings}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Selection guides, gradients propose, correctors revise ---
and each pays only in its regime} (\S\ref{sec:ablations}): recovery needs
only twisted selection; annealing and the corrector matter for far-source
editing; the tilt matters when the target demands low-prior vocabulary.
Failure modes addressed: coverage (tilt), expressivity (canvas width),
irreversibility (corrector), premature collapse (annealing).
\item \textbf{Distilled samplers make mixtures unreachable}
(\S\ref{sec:distill}); gradient-free guidance can swap them out at zero
memory cost --- a structural advantage over backprop-through-$f_\phi$
methods.
\item \textbf{Estimator choice inverts with scale}: the unbiased sampled
twist wins in the toy world; the biased-but-coherent mode twist wins with
real LMs, at a fraction of the cost.
\item \textbf{Pure first-order discrete guidance fails} where
nominate-then-exact-score succeeds --- relevant to D-CBG-style methods.
\item \textbf{Compositional vs.\ distributional straddling}: CLIP-MMD
partially accepts ``both modes in one image'' as a substitute for ``a
population mixing two modes''.
\item \textbf{Selection over a population survives vocabulary scale;
locally tilted rates do not} (\S\ref{sec:ctmc}): rate-ratio guidance
matches SMC in small alphabets at lower cost, but at 18k-word vocabulary
it is dominated at every $K$/restart split of the budget --- the missing
ingredient is resampling, not estimate precision, and it cannot be
imitated for free: remasking guided by cached commit-time regret is
indistinguishable from uniform churn.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations and next steps}
Most results use 3 seeds on few targets; the corrector's effective prior
is an approximation of the masked-diffusion prior
(\S\ref{sec:corrector}); far-source editing stalls above the floor;
prompts are optimal but not always fluent (except under LLaDA).
Next: more targets (in particular low-prior-vocabulary multimodal targets,
where the ablation predicts the tilt becomes load-bearing), a remasking
corrector with ReMDM guarantees, and continuous-time formalism for the
guidance theory.
